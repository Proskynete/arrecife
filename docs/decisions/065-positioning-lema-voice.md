# § 65 · One lema, one bajada, and a voice that is his and not the manual's

**Release:** unreleased · [index](README.md)

---

**System rule:** the manual's subtitle reads «Engineering leadership, plataforma
y la era de la IA». The full lockup carries the line `code · build · explore`.
The merch table prints «FILTRA EL RUIDO» on the black tee and `BUILD · LEARN ·
SHARE` on the sand one. § 08 describes the voice with «humor seco» and «nada de
emoji en material de marca».

**What there was:** not one positioning but five. The projects said «Frontend
Architect & Technical Lead» in some places and «Engineering Leadership &
Platform Thinking» in others; LinkedIn said «Technical Lead – Ayudando a los
equipos a trabajar con IA | Spec-Driven Development»; Instagram said
«Engineering Leadership · Platform Thinking · Ayudo equipos de ingeniería a
escalar en la era del AI». And three lemas lived side by side in the manual
itself. Each of them was defensible. Together they said nobody in particular.

**How it was decided:** an interview with the author, five blocks — who he is,
what he does and for whom, what he thinks, how he wants to be perceived, how he
talks — answered in his own words. Two answers moved everything:

- Asked which topic he would drop, he dropped **leadership**: «siento que es muy
  abstracto y no sé si tenga las competencias formales como para enseñarlo».
  Leadership is his job; it is not what the brand should sell. So «Engineering
  Leadership» cannot be the headline — it would be selling the one thing he does
  not claim.
- Asked what he would keep doing unpaid, the answer was **teaching**. The topic
  today is Spec-Driven Development; the constant is that he explains things.

And one word ran through all five blocks: *entender*. The curious kid with
Windows 95, «un buen desarrollador es el que entiende el problema», the refusal
to vibecode without «el conocimiento cabal de lo que hace el agente», teaching
concepts before frameworks.

**What it is now:**

Retired as positioning: «Frontend Architect», «Engineering Leadership»,
«Platform Thinking», «Senior Software Engineer enfocado en frontend»,
`code · build · explore`, `BUILD · LEARN · SHARE`, «FILTRA EL RUIDO». A CV line
saying he worked as a frontend engineer is history, not positioning, and stays.

**The voice, as he described it** — which is not what § 08 says. He read that
section and said it is not his tone:

- Neutral Spanish. No regionalisms in public material, Chilean or otherwise.
- First person. Experience is told as «yo hice» or «hicimos con mi equipo». A
  warning includes him: «nos va a pasar esto si no hacemos esto otro». Never
  the «tú deberías» from above.
- The complex made simple, with a concrete example before any abstraction. The
  style he rejects by name is the one that makes everything sound hard and
  reaches for rebuscado words to look intellectual.
- What he fears most is being taken for a «vende humo». What he wants a talk to
  leave behind: it was playful, it held attention, and people learned a lot.
- On AI: it is a tool, the engineer gives it direction and owns the result — the
  tool is not to blame when the house comes out badly built.
- **Emoji: few, not none.** Sparingly on Instagram and LinkedIn, where they
  belong to the platform. None in articles, slides or printed pieces.

The detailed writing voice is being rebuilt in `blog-content-manager` from his
own prose (the `author-voice-profile` corpus). That corpus is the source of
truth for how he writes; § 08 should summarise brand-level rules and point there
instead of describing a voice of its own.

**Why the merch table is left half empty.** The only approved merch text is the
lema, and it goes on the black tee in place of «FILTRA EL RUIDO». Every other
phrase proposed in the interview was rejected by the author as too generic, and
the manual's own rule — «nadie pega un sticker por el nombre» — is the reason not
to fill the slot with a placeholder that sounds like anyone. The sand tee's
phrase is **pending**, and it is written that way rather than guessed.
